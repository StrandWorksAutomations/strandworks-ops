# Proposed structural anatomy course roadmap

## Audience and purpose

Design for health-professions students learning anatomy for the first time. The learner builds a regional mental map through naming, localization, relationships and changed-view practice. The course supplies its own entry orientation and remediation.

The institutional use case remains a hypothesis to evaluate with educators. This packet makes no school endorsement, accreditation, CE credit or classroom-effectiveness claim.

## Proposed series

| Course concept | Intended learning scope | Current boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Upper-limb skeleton** | Shoulder girdle through the hand, ending in integrated identification and relationships. | First course; shoulder lesson authored for review. Static bilateral bones. |
| **2. Lower-limb skeleton** | Hip bones through the foot. | Outline only; no inferred joint movement or missing soft tissues. |
| **3. Axial skeleton and thoracic relationships** | Vertebral regions, ribs, sternum parts and skull structures. | Outline only; lesson-specific geometry gaps need review. |
| **4. Major vessels by region** | Named vessel identity and regional spatial relationships. | Outline only; source geometry does not establish a complete continuous flow network. |
| **5. Selected abdominal and pelvic structures** | Relationships among available organs, parts, vessels and bones. | Outline only; selective coverage, not complete organ anatomy. |
| **6. Muscles and peripheral nerves** | Additional structures and neurovascular relationships. | Future audited asset work; movement needs a separately reviewed model. |

The registered atlas has 202 skeletal, 627 vascular and 47 selected organ/organ-part structures. These are source registrations, not a complete-body inventory. No missing structure should be invented to fill a lesson.

## First course: Upper-limb skeleton

| Lesson | Main contribution | Connection to earlier learning | Development state |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shoulder girdle** | Establish the region, person-side/view reference, primary bones and taught connections. | Provides beginner orientation. | Authored plan and local narrated demonstration. |
| **Humerus** | Extend the regional map into the arm. | Retrieve the shoulder relationship before adding content. | Proposed. |
| **Radius and ulna** | Distinguish the two forearm bones in the anatomical reference position. | Retrieve the arm map and directional vocabulary. | Proposed. |
| **Carpal groups** | Organize the reviewed taught wrist set. | Retrieve forearm-to-wrist context. | Proposed; scope follows source/geometry review. |
| **Metacarpals** | Extend wrist-to-hand organization and associate structures with digits. | Reconstruct the prior grouping. | Proposed. |
| **Digits and integrated review** | Combine shoulder, arm, forearm, wrist and hand identification. | Retrieve the whole regional sequence. | Proposed. |

Course duration follows the final objectives and observed practice load. Earlier 40-minute course and seven-minute sample estimates were not accepted learning-time targets. The current narration is about 130 seconds; a full lesson also includes learner-paced tasks. Actual lesson duration has not been established with intended learners.

## Consistency across the sequence

Each lesson uses Orient, Explain, Predict, Guided practice, Independent transfer and Recap. Earlier material returns in purposeful retrieval checks with feedback. The final review combines taught outcomes rather than introducing an unexpected terminology list.

Naming, side and localization are recorded separately. A highlighted unnamed target may test naming; a prompt giving the name tests localization. A correction after revealing help remains assisted evidence. No arbitrary pass percentage or competence claim follows from completion.

## Scope and packaging

The upper-limb course adds sustained regional learning beyond the existing introductory anatomy lessons. It should not repackage those introductions as the whole paid course. The standalone course provides its own orientation; a separate Foundations purchase is not a proposed prerequisite.

Future course pricing and purchase implementation are separate from this content review. No final course price, entitlement change or production release is established by this packet.

## Review progression

1. Review the shared lesson contract and the complete shoulder storyboard.
2. Check anatomy, wording, sources, geometry and answer concealment with independent reviewers.
3. Review actual voice, model framing and learner flow in the local demonstration.
4. Observe intended beginners, inspect confusion/help needed and revise.
5. Approve production app integration and distribution separately.
