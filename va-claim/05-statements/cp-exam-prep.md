# Compensation & Pension (C&P) exam — prep

VA schedules it (often through a contractor: LHI/Optum, QTC, VES). The
letter or text gives date, place, examiner. **Missing it is a denial.**
Reschedule through the contractor's number on the notice if unavoidable.

## Before

- Re-read the personal statement the night before; the exam should match
  it. Bring a copy of it and of the nexus letter/DBQ in case the examiner
  has not seen the file (they should have; do not assume).
- Write a one-page symptom list: each symptom, how often, how bad, an
  example. Panic, sleep, mood, irritability, concentration, memory,
  withdrawal, hypervigilance, any suicidal thoughts (and when), substance
  use, effect on work and family.
- Do not "clean up" for the exam: no extra preparation, no best-day
  presentation. The examiner records how you present.

## During

- Answer the question asked; then give the example. Frequency and
  severity words matter: "most nights", "two or three times a week",
  "every shift".
- Describe the worst weeks, not the average, when asked "how are you
  doing" — and say that you are describing the bad periods.
- The exam is usually 30–60 minutes. If it is 10 minutes and skips the
  history, note the time and tell the VSO; an inadequate exam can be
  challenged.
- If asked about the 2004 discharge: state the facts (age, timing, no
  prior history, what you were told) and that the current diagnosis is
  [X]. Do not argue law with the examiner.
- Tell them about work honestly: what it costs, not just that you manage.

## After

- Same day, write down what was asked, what you said, and how long it
  lasted. Save it in Drive.
- The exam report appears in the claim file; the VSO can read it before
  the rating decision and, if it is deficient, request clarification.
