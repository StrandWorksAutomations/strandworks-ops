# Request to a clinician for a nexus opinion (+ DBQ)

Give this page, the long-copy DD214, the separation packet (once obtained),
the entrance-exam pages, and any prior treatment records to the evaluating
psychologist or psychiatrist. Ask for two documents: (1) the completed
public **Mental Disorders DBQ** (va.gov → "Disability Benefits
Questionnaires"), and (2) a separate signed opinion letter on letterhead
covering the points below. A letter that skips any of these is routinely
discounted.

## What the letter must contain

1. **Credentials** and a statement of what was reviewed (list each document,
   including the 2004 separation packet and the DD214, and the interview
   date and length).
2. **Current diagnosis** (DSM-5-TR), with the symptoms supporting it and
   their frequency/severity.
3. **History**, in the clinician's words: pre-service baseline; the 2004
   in-service symptoms as recorded by the Army; the course since.
4. **The opinion, in VA's language:** "It is at least as likely as not
   (50 percent or greater probability) that the veteran's [diagnosis] had
   its onset during his active military service in 2004 and has continued
   since."
5. **Direct discussion of the 2004 personality-disorder diagnosis:** why,
   in the clinician's judgment, the symptoms documented in 2004 are better
   explained by an acquired psychiatric disorder (or represent an acquired
   disorder superimposed on any personality pathology). Address: the
   veteran's age (18), the short time in service, the absence of any
   pre-service history, the diagnostic requirements for a personality
   disorder (enduring pattern, long duration), and whether the 2004
   evaluation met them and who performed it.
6. **Pre-existence:** an explicit statement whether there is any evidence
   the condition existed before December 2003, and if the clinician
   believes it did not, say so. If they think it might have, the letter
   must then address whether service aggravated it beyond natural
   progression.
7. **Rationale** — the "because" for every conclusion. VA weighs reasoning,
   not credentials alone (Nieves-Rodriguez v. Peake, 22 Vet. App. 295).
8. **Occupational and social impairment** — a sentence or two tying the
   symptoms to work and relationships (this drives the rating).
9. Signature, license number, date.

## What to avoid

- "Possibly", "may be", "could be related" — these do not meet the
  standard. Use "at least as likely as not" or "more likely than not".
- Opinions that rely only on the veteran's report without engaging the
  service records.
- Ignoring the personality-disorder diagnosis; the examiner will not.
